# 13. Target Assets

## Introduction

Target assets are the investment destinations of carry capital.

After capital is borrowed using a funding currency, investors deploy those funds into assets expected to generate superior risk-adjusted returns.

The choice of target assets determines the potential profitability of the carry trade and influences the direction of global capital flows.

Within GCFIOS, target assets are viewed as the recipients of global liquidity and represent the final destination of carry capital after it leaves the funding market.

---

## Definition

A target asset is any financial asset or investment opportunity that receives borrowed capital because it is expected to provide returns that exceed the total cost of funding.

Returns may be generated through:

• Interest income

• Bond yields

• Dividend income

• Capital appreciation

• Commodity appreciation

• Currency appreciation

• Credit spreads

• Alternative investment returns

The objective is to maximise returns while maintaining acceptable levels of risk.

---

## Characteristics Of Attractive Target Assets

Assets commonly selected for carry strategies generally possess several characteristics:

• Higher expected returns

• Strong liquidity

• Stable financial markets

• Healthy economic outlook

• Institutional participation

• Attractive risk-adjusted performance

• Efficient market access

The combination of these characteristics encourages institutional capital allocation.

---

## Categories Of Target Assets

Carry capital may be allocated into numerous asset classes.

Examples include:

### High-Yielding Currencies

Currencies offering higher interest rates relative to funding currencies.

---

### Government Bonds

Sovereign debt offering attractive yields with acceptable levels of risk.

---

### Corporate Bonds

Investment-grade and high-yield corporate debt providing enhanced income opportunities.

---

### Equity Markets

Broad equity indices and individual companies expected to benefit from improving liquidity and economic growth.

---

### Growth Assets

Technology companies, innovation sectors, and other growth-oriented investments that benefit from abundant liquidity.

---

### Commodities

Industrial and energy commodities that may appreciate alongside improving economic conditions.

---

### Emerging Markets

Emerging-market currencies, sovereign debt, equities, and corporate credit that attract international capital seeking higher returns.

---

### Alternative Investments

Private equity

Infrastructure

Real estate

Private credit

Hedge fund strategies

Other institutional investment opportunities

---

## Factors Influencing Target Asset Selection

Institutional investors evaluate multiple factors before selecting target assets.

Examples include:

• Expected return

• Risk-adjusted performance

• Liquidity

• Market depth

• Credit quality

• Political stability

• Economic growth

• Monetary policy

• Exchange-rate expectations

• Regulatory environment

Target asset selection is therefore a comprehensive capital allocation decision rather than a search for yield alone.

---

## Risks Associated With Target Assets

Target assets remain exposed to multiple risks.

Examples include:

• Falling asset prices

• Declining liquidity

• Currency depreciation

• Rising interest rates

• Credit deterioration

• Political instability

• Economic slowdown

• Geopolitical events

• Regulatory changes

Changes in these conditions may reduce expected returns and encourage capital reallocation.

---

## Target Assets During Carry Expansion

When carry conditions improve:

• Institutional allocations increase.

• Liquidity expands.

• Asset demand rises.

• Market participation broadens.

• Investor confidence strengthens.

Target assets frequently experience improving financial conditions during this phase.

---

## Target Assets During Carry Contraction

When carry conditions weaken:

• New capital inflows slow.

• Existing positions become more selective.

• Liquidity expansion moderates.

• Price appreciation slows.

Some target assets begin to underperform before broader market deterioration becomes apparent.

---

## Target Assets During Carry Unwind

During carry unwinds:

• Existing positions are reduced.

• Institutional investors liquidate assets.

• Capital returns to funding markets.

• Liquidity contracts.

• Volatility increases.

• Asset prices frequently decline.

Assets that previously received the largest carry inflows often experience the largest capital outflows.

## GCFIOS Interpretation

Within GCFIOS, target assets represent the destination of international capital allocation.

The objective is not merely to identify attractive investments, but to determine where global liquidity is most likely to flow under current macroeconomic conditions.

Changes in monetary policy, liquidity, funding costs, credit conditions, volatility, and investor confidence all influence the attractiveness of target assets.

Target asset analysis therefore supports the Pressure Engine, Transmission Engine, Environment Engine, Alignment Engine, Portfolio Risk Engine, and Trade Management Engine by identifying where institutional capital is most likely to be deployed or withdrawn as global carry conditions evolve.
