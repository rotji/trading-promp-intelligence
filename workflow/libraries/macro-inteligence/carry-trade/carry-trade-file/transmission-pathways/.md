# 7. Transmission Pathways

## Introduction

Carry trades do not affect only the currencies involved in the transaction.

They redistribute capital throughout the global financial system.

Every carry trade represents the movement of liquidity from one financial environment into another.

As this capital moves, it influences funding markets, foreign exchange, government bonds, corporate credit, equity markets, commodities, cryptocurrencies, and broader investor behaviour.

Within GCFIOS, carry trades are therefore viewed as one of the primary mechanisms through which global liquidity and macroeconomic pressures are transmitted across financial markets.

---

## Fundamental Principle

Carry trades transmit capital.

Capital transmission produces liquidity changes.

Liquidity changes influence asset prices.

Therefore:

Carry Trade

↓

Capital Flow

↓

Liquidity Change

↓

Asset Price Movement

↓

Cross-Asset Transmission

---

## Stage 1 — Funding Market

The transmission process begins in the funding market.

Investors obtain low-cost capital from a funding currency.

Typical characteristics include:

• Low policy rates

• Deep funding markets

• Stable borrowing conditions

• High liquidity

The funding market becomes the origin of capital movement.

---

## Stage 2 — Currency Conversion

Borrowed funds are converted into the target currency.

This creates:

• Selling pressure on the funding currency.

• Buying pressure on the target currency.

As carry participation increases, exchange-rate movements begin reflecting these capital flows.

---

## Stage 3 — Capital Deployment

After conversion, capital is invested into higher-return opportunities.

Examples include:

• Government bonds

• Corporate bonds

• Equities

• Commodities

• Real estate

• Infrastructure

• Emerging markets

• Cryptocurrencies

Capital has now entered productive investment markets.

---

## Stage 4 — Liquidity Expansion

As investment increases:

• Asset demand rises.

• Market liquidity improves.

• Credit conditions often become more supportive.

• Financial conditions become easier.

This stage represents the liquidity expansion effect of carry trades.

---

## Stage 5 — Asset Price Response

Increasing liquidity often supports:

• Equity appreciation

• Bond demand

• Higher-yield currencies

• Commodity demand

• Credit market strength

• Improved market breadth

The exact response depends on prevailing macroeconomic conditions.

---

## Stage 6 — Feedback Loop

As asset prices rise:

• Portfolio values increase.

• Investor confidence improves.

• Risk appetite strengthens.

• Additional carry trades become attractive.

This creates a positive feedback loop that may further reinforce carry expansion.

## Global Liquidity Transmission

Within GCFIOS, carry trades are recognised as one of the principal channels through which global liquidity moves between countries.

Carry activity therefore affects:

• International capital allocation

• Funding availability

• Portfolio positioning

• Cross-border investment

• Systemic financial conditions

Changes in carry conditions rarely remain isolated within one market.

They propagate throughout the global financial system.

---

## GCFIOS Interpretation

Within GCFIOS, transmission analysis is more important than the carry trade itself.

The objective is not merely to identify that a carry trade exists.

The objective is to understand how that carry trade redistributes liquidity, alters capital flows, changes investor behaviour, and ultimately affects multiple asset classes simultaneously.

For this reason, carry transmission becomes a foundational input into the Pressure Engine, Transmission Engine, Environment Engine, Alignment Engine, Portfolio Risk Engine, and Trade Management Engine.

Carry trades are therefore analysed not as isolated strategies, but as dynamic global capital-flow transmission mechanisms capable of influencing the entire financial system.
