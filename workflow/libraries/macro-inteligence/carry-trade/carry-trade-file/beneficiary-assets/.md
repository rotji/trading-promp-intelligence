# 8. Beneficiary Assets

## Introduction

Carry trades redistribute capital toward assets that are expected to generate superior risk-adjusted returns.

As carry activity expands, liquidity flows into markets that offer attractive yield, growth potential, or capital appreciation.

Within GCFIOS, beneficiary assets are not identified solely by historical performance.

Instead, they are identified by understanding where global capital is most likely to flow under favourable carry conditions.

The exact beneficiaries vary depending on the prevailing macroeconomic environment, monetary policy, and investor preferences.

---

## Fundamental Principle

Carry expansion encourages capital to move toward assets offering:

• Higher expected returns

• Attractive yield

• Strong economic prospects

• Healthy liquidity

• Acceptable levels of risk

These assets generally receive increased capital inflows during periods of expanding carry activity.

---

## 1. Higher-Yielding Currencies

Currencies with relatively higher interest rates often benefit from carry expansion.

Reasons include:

• Positive interest-rate differentials

• Attractive yield opportunities

• Increased foreign capital inflows

Typical characteristics:

• Strong policy rates

• Stable monetary policy

• Healthy financial markets

---

## 2. Equity Markets

Equity markets frequently benefit from expanding carry trades.

Additional liquidity supports:

• Higher investor participation

• Rising valuations

• Improved market breadth

Growth-oriented equity markets often attract significant carry capital.

---

## 3. Growth Stocks

Technology and other high-growth sectors often perform well when carry conditions are favourable.

Reasons include:

• Improved liquidity

• Lower financing stress

• Strong investor confidence

Growth assets generally benefit from expanding global risk appetite.

---

## 4. Government Bonds

Government bonds offering attractive yields may receive carry-related capital inflows.

Institutional investors often allocate capital toward sovereign debt that provides favourable returns relative to funding costs.

---

## 5. Corporate Credit

Investment-grade and high-yield corporate bonds may benefit when carry expansion improves liquidity and investor confidence.

Supportive carry conditions often reduce financing stress within credit markets.

---

## 6. Emerging Markets

Emerging-market assets frequently attract capital during carry expansion.

Potential beneficiaries include:

• Emerging-market currencies

• Sovereign bonds

• Equity markets

• Corporate debt

These markets often experience stronger inflows when global investors seek higher returns.

---

## 7. Commodities

Certain commodities may benefit indirectly through:

• Stronger global growth

• Improved industrial demand

• Expanding investment activity

Commodity performance depends on the broader macroeconomic environment.

---

## 8. Cryptocurrencies

Digital assets may benefit during periods of abundant liquidity and strong risk appetite.

Although cryptocurrencies do not generate traditional yield, expanding carry conditions often encourage investors to increase exposure to higher-risk assets.

---

## 9. Real Assets

Carry expansion may also support investment into:

• Infrastructure

• Commercial real estate

• Private equity

• Long-duration investment projects

Improved financing conditions make these investments more attractive.

---

## Typical Characteristics Of Beneficiary Assets

Assets benefiting from carry expansion often exhibit:

• Strong liquidity

• Positive growth expectations

• Higher expected returns

• Healthy capital inflows

• Strong institutional participation

• Positive market momentum

---

## Factors Affecting Beneficiary Selection

Not every asset benefits equally.

Capital allocation depends on:

• Monetary policy

• Interest-rate differentials

• Economic growth

• Credit conditions

• Market liquidity

• Political stability

• Investor confidence

These factors determine where capital is ultimately deployed.

## GCFIOS Interpretation

Within GCFIOS, beneficiary assets represent the destinations of global carry capital.

The objective is not to assume that every risk asset will rise.

Instead, GCFIOS evaluates which asset classes are most likely to receive capital under the current macroeconomic environment.

Carry expansion therefore increases the probability that beneficiary assets will experience improving liquidity, stronger capital inflows, and more favourable market conditions.

The final determination depends on the interaction between carry conditions, broader macro pressures, market structure, and asset-specific characteristics.
