# 12. Funding Currencies

## Introduction

Every carry trade begins with a funding currency.

The funding currency is the currency in which capital is borrowed before being invested into higher-returning assets.

The characteristics of the funding currency largely determine the cost of financing and therefore play a critical role in the profitability, sustainability, and risk of carry trades.

Within GCFIOS, funding currencies are viewed as the origin of global carry capital and one of the primary sources of international liquidity transmission.

---

## Definition

A funding currency is a currency that investors borrow because the cost of obtaining capital in that currency is relatively low.

The borrowed funds are then converted into another currency or invested into assets expected to generate a higher return.

The profitability of a carry trade depends partly on maintaining a sufficiently low funding cost throughout the investment period.

---

## Characteristics Of A Good Funding Currency

An effective funding currency typically possesses several characteristics:

• Low interest rates

• Stable monetary policy

• Deep and liquid financial markets

• Reliable access to funding

• High market confidence

• Low funding volatility

• Strong institutional participation

These characteristics encourage large-scale borrowing by institutional investors.

---

## Why Certain Currencies Become Funding Currencies

A currency generally becomes a funding currency because borrowing costs remain consistently lower than those available elsewhere.

Contributing factors include:

• Accommodative central bank policy

• Low inflation

• Stable domestic financial conditions

• Large and liquid money markets

• Well-developed banking systems

These conditions make borrowing both inexpensive and operationally efficient.

---

## Historical Funding Currencies

Historically, several currencies have frequently served as funding currencies.

Examples include:

• Japanese Yen (JPY)

• Swiss Franc (CHF)

In different periods, other currencies have also been used depending on prevailing monetary policy and funding conditions.

The identity of the dominant funding currency may therefore change over time.

---

## Funding Currency Life Cycle

A funding currency may progress through several stages:

Low Interest Rates

↓

Cheap Funding

↓

Institutional Borrowing

↓

Carry Trade Expansion

↓

Policy Changes

↓

Funding Costs Increase

↓

Carry Contraction

↓

Carry Unwind

↓

Funding Conditions Stabilise

↓

Potential Future Funding Currency

The life cycle reflects the evolving role of a currency within global capital markets.

---

## Factors That Influence Funding Currency Status

Several macroeconomic factors influence whether a currency functions as a funding currency.

Examples include:

• Central bank policy

• Policy interest rates

• Inflation

• Liquidity conditions

• Banking system stability

• Credit market conditions

• Exchange-rate expectations

• Global investor confidence

Changes in these factors may strengthen or weaken a currency's attractiveness as a funding source.

---

## Risks Associated With Funding Currencies

Funding currencies are not risk-free.

Potential risks include:

• Rising policy rates

• Unexpected monetary tightening

• Currency appreciation

• Liquidity shortages

• Funding market disruptions

• Political or geopolitical uncertainty

• Banking system stress

These risks can reduce carry profitability and increase the probability of carry contraction or unwind.

---

## Funding Currency During Carry Expansion

During carry expansion:

• Borrowing increases.

• Funding currency is sold.

• Capital flows into higher-return assets.

• Global liquidity expands.

Funding currencies often experience downward pressure as borrowing activity increases.

---

## Funding Currency During Carry Unwind

During carry unwinds:

• Carry positions are closed.

• Borrowed capital is repaid.

• Funding currency is repurchased.

• Funding currency often appreciates.

• Global liquidity contracts.

This reversal explains why funding currencies frequently strengthen during periods of financial stress.

---

## GCFIOS Interpretation

Within GCFIOS, funding currencies are monitored continuously because they represent the starting point of global carry transmission.

Changes in funding costs, monetary policy, liquidity conditions, or investor behaviour may alter the attractiveness of borrowing and significantly influence global capital flows.

Funding currency analysis therefore contributes directly to the Pressure Engine, Transmission Engine, Environment Engine, Alignment Engine, Portfolio Risk Engine, and Trade Management Engine.

Rather than viewing funding currencies simply as low-interest-rate currencies, GCFIOS interprets them as dynamic sources of global liquidity whose changing conditions influence capital allocation across multiple asset classes and financial markets.
