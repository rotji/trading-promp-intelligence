> **What causes carry trades to weaken before they completely unwind?**

This is different from an unwind.

# 5. Contraction Conditions

## Introduction

Carry trade contraction occurs when the conditions that previously supported carry strategies begin to deteriorate.

Unlike a carry unwind, contraction does not necessarily involve rapid liquidation of existing positions.

Instead, it represents a gradual reduction in the attractiveness of carry trades as funding conditions, expected returns, or market confidence begin to weaken.

Within GCFIOS, carry contraction is interpreted as an early warning phase that may eventually progress into a full carry unwind if deterioration continues.

---

## Fundamental Principle

Carry trades contract when:

The expected reward from holding carry positions begins to decline relative to the risks involved.

As the balance between risk and reward becomes less favourable, investors gradually reduce new carry positions and become more selective in deploying capital.

---

## 1. Rising Funding Costs

Carry profitability declines when borrowing becomes more expensive.

Funding costs may increase because of:

• Higher central bank policy rates

• Rising money market rates

• Tighter repo funding

• Higher financing costs

As funding becomes more expensive, fewer carry opportunities remain attractive.

---

## 2. Narrowing Interest Rate Differentials

Carry trades rely on positive interest-rate spreads.

Contraction occurs when:

• Funding-country rates rise

or

• Target-country rates fall

or

• Both occur simultaneously.

Smaller differentials reduce the incentive to maintain carry positions.

---

## 3. Monetary Policy Convergence

Carry opportunities often weaken when central banks begin moving toward similar policy settings.

Examples include:

• Funding central banks raising rates.

• Target central banks cutting rates.

• Simultaneous reduction in policy divergence.

As policy differences narrow, carry opportunities become less attractive.

---

## 4. Declining Global Liquidity

Carry contraction is frequently associated with tightening liquidity conditions.

Examples include:

• Reduced bank lending

• Funding market stress

• Lower reserve availability

• Higher financing costs

Reduced liquidity limits the ability of investors to expand leveraged carry positions.

---

## 5. Weakening Risk Appetite

Carry trades depend on investor confidence.

As uncertainty increases, investors become less willing to allocate capital toward higher-risk assets.

Signs include:

• Reduced equity exposure

• Lower credit demand

• Declining emerging-market investment

• Increasing preference for defensive assets

---

## 6. Increasing Market Volatility

Higher volatility increases uncertainty and raises the probability of adverse price movements.

As volatility rises:

• Leverage becomes less attractive.

• Risk management becomes more restrictive.

• Carry returns become less predictable.

Many institutions therefore reduce carry exposure before volatility becomes extreme.

---

## 7. Increasing Exchange Rate Risk

Carry profitability depends partly on stable currency relationships.

Contraction is encouraged when exchange-rate uncertainty increases.

Greater currency volatility raises the probability that exchange-rate losses will offset carry income.

---

## 8. Tightening Credit Conditions

Carry activity often declines when access to financing becomes more restrictive.

Examples include:

• Wider credit spreads

• Reduced lending activity

• Tighter financing standards

• Declining market liquidity

More restrictive credit conditions reduce investors' willingness to employ leverage.

---

## 9. Deteriorating Economic Outlook

Slowing economic growth reduces confidence in higher-risk investments.

Examples include:

• Weakening economic activity

• Declining corporate earnings

• Recession concerns

• Falling business confidence

These conditions reduce the expected return from many carry investments.

---

## 10. Reduced Policy Confidence

Carry contraction may occur when investors become uncertain about future central-bank actions.

Examples include:

• Unexpected policy guidance

• Increased uncertainty around future interest rates

• Conflicting economic signals

Policy uncertainty often encourages investors to reduce exposure before major decisions are made.

---

## Carry Contraction Transmission

Funding Costs Rise

↓

Carry Profitability Declines

↓

New Carry Positions Slow

↓

Existing Positions Reduced

↓

Liquidity Growth Weakens

↓

Risk Appetite Softens

↓

Capital Becomes More Selective

↓

Carry Contraction

---

## Typical Market Characteristics

Carry contraction is commonly associated with:

• Slower capital allocation

• Reduced leverage

• Increasing caution

• Narrowing market leadership

• Moderating liquidity expansion

• Increasing defensive positioning

Unlike a carry unwind, financial markets may remain orderly during contraction.

The adjustment is often gradual rather than abrupt.

---

## Relationship To Carry Unwind

Carry contraction is not the same as a carry unwind.

Carry contraction represents:

• A gradual weakening of supportive conditions.

Carry unwind represents:

• Active liquidation of existing carry positions.

A prolonged contraction may eventually develop into a carry unwind if financial conditions continue to deteriorate.

---

## GCFIOS Interpretation

Within GCFIOS, carry contraction is interpreted as a transition phase between carry expansion and carry unwind.

The system monitors changes in funding costs, interest-rate differentials, liquidity, volatility, exchange-rate stability, credit conditions, and investor confidence to determine whether carry conditions are merely weakening or approaching a more significant unwind.

Carry contraction therefore serves as an important early warning signal that global liquidity expansion may be slowing and that risk assets could become increasingly vulnerable if deterioration continues.
