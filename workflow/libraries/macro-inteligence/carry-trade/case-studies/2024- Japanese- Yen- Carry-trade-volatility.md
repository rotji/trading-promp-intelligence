# 2024 Japanese Yen Carry Trade Volatility

Event Date:

July – August 2024

Category:

• Carry Trade Unwind

• BOJ Policy Shock

• Funding Currency Event

• Global Volatility Event

========================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
========================================================

During mid-2024, global financial markets experienced one of the most significant episodes of Japanese Yen carry trade volatility in recent years.

For decades, Japan's extremely low interest rates had made the Japanese Yen the world's dominant funding currency.

Institutional investors borrowed Yen at very low cost and invested the proceeds into higher-yielding assets including US equities, US Treasuries, corporate bonds, emerging markets, commodities, and other global investments.

This strategy remained highly profitable while Japanese interest rates stayed extremely low and the Yen remained weak.

However, during 2024 the Bank of Japan gradually moved away from decades of ultra-loose monetary policy.

Markets began anticipating additional interest-rate increases and reduced monetary accommodation.

At the same time, the Japanese Yen began strengthening.

These developments significantly reduced the attractiveness of borrowing Yen for carry trades.

Institutional investors started reducing carry positions.

As Yen appreciation accelerated, leveraged investors experienced losses from currency movements in addition to funding costs.

Many institutions reduced leverage and unwound positions across multiple asset classes.

The result was a synchronized increase in volatility across foreign exchange, equities, bonds, and other risk assets.

Although the event did not become a global financial crisis comparable to 1998 or 2008, it demonstrated how changes in a single funding currency can influence global capital allocation and increase market volatility worldwide.

========================================================
LESSONS LEARNED
========================================================

This event demonstrated that:

• Funding currencies can rapidly influence global markets.

• Small changes in funding costs may trigger large capital reallocations.

• Crowded positioning magnifies volatility.

• BOJ policy is globally important despite Japan's relatively small economic growth.

• Carry profitability must always be evaluated alongside currency movements.

• Carry contractions often begin gradually before becoming visible across multiple asset classes.

========================================================
GCFIOS IMPLICATIONS
========================================================

The 2024 Yen carry event reinforces the importance of continuously monitoring:

• BOJ policy.

• Japanese interest rates.

• JGB yields.

• Yen exchange rates.

• Carry profitability.

• Funding conditions.

• Institutional positioning.

• Global volatility.

It also demonstrates that funding currencies should never be analysed independently from monetary policy, capital flows, and investor positioning.

Most Important Lesson:

The world's dominant funding currency does not need to experience a financial crisis to disrupt global markets. Even gradual changes in Bank of Japan policy can alter funding costs, reduce carry profitability, trigger institutional deleveraging, and increase volatility across multiple asset classes. Continuous monitoring of funding currencies is therefore essential for understanding future global capital flows.
