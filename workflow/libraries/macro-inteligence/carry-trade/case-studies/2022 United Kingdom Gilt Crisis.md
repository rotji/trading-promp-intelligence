# 2022 United Kingdom Gilt Crisis

Event Date:

September – October 2022

Category:

• Sovereign Bond Crisis

• Liquidity Crisis

• Pension Fund Crisis

• Leveraged Carry Trade Event

• Financial Stability Intervention

========================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
========================================================

The 2022 United Kingdom Gilt Crisis demonstrated how leverage within government bond markets can rapidly evolve into a funding and liquidity crisis even without a banking collapse.

Following the announcement of the United Kingdom's "Mini Budget" in September 2022, financial markets questioned the sustainability of future government borrowing.

Government bond yields rose dramatically within days.

Because many UK pension funds employed Liability-Driven Investment (LDI) strategies that relied heavily on leverage and derivatives, the rapid rise in gilt yields generated enormous collateral calls.

Pension funds were forced to sell government bonds in order to raise cash.

Those sales pushed yields even higher.

Higher yields generated additional margin calls.

The cycle became self-reinforcing.

Rather than being caused by poor credit quality, the crisis resulted from excessive leverage, inadequate liquidity planning, and forced deleveraging within institutional portfolios.

Concern grew that the dysfunction could spread throughout the UK financial system.

The Bank of England intervened by temporarily purchasing long-dated government bonds to restore orderly market functioning.

The intervention stabilized liquidity and prevented broader systemic contagion.

The crisis demonstrated that even government bond markets can become illiquid when leveraged investors are forced to liquidate positions simultaneously.

========================================================
LESSONS LEARNED
========================================================

The UK Gilt Crisis demonstrated that:

• Government bond markets are not immune to liquidity crises.

• Leverage within pension funds can create systemic risk.

• Margin calls can accelerate market instability.

• Fiscal policy can rapidly influence funding conditions.

• Central-bank intervention may be required even when banks are not the source of the crisis.

• Liquidity risk can become more important than credit risk.

========================================================
