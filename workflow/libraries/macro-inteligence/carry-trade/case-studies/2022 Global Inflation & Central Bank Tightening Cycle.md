========================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
========================================================

The 2022 Global Inflation and Central Bank Tightening Cycle marked one of the most coordinated global monetary tightening episodes in decades.

Following the unprecedented monetary and fiscal stimulus introduced during the COVID-19 pandemic, inflation accelerated sharply across advanced and emerging economies.

Supply-chain disruptions, energy shortages, strong consumer demand, labor market tightness, and geopolitical tensions contributed to inflation reaching levels not seen for decades.

In response, central banks around the world shifted from highly accommodative monetary policies to aggressive interest-rate increases.

The Federal Reserve led one of the fastest tightening cycles in modern history.

The European Central Bank, Bank of England, Reserve Bank of Australia, Bank of Canada, and many other central banks also raised interest rates significantly.

Although the Bank of Japan maintained relatively accommodative policy for much of this period, the global funding environment became considerably less supportive.

Higher funding costs reduced leverage, tightened financial conditions, slowed global liquidity growth, and gradually reduced the attractiveness of many carry strategies.

Unlike the crises of 1998, 2008, or 2020, this period did not produce an immediate systemic liquidity collapse.

Instead, carry conditions weakened progressively as monetary policy became increasingly restrictive.

Financial markets adjusted through declining valuations, increased volatility, slowing credit growth, and more selective capital allocation.

The tightening cycle demonstrated that carry trades can contract over extended periods without producing an immediate financial crisis.

========================================================
LESSONS LEARNED
========================================================

The global tightening cycle demonstrated that:

• Carry trades do not require a financial crisis to weaken.

• Rising funding costs gradually reduce carry profitability.

• Inflation can become the dominant driver of global capital flows.

• Central-bank policy coordination can reshape global liquidity.

• Liquidity-sensitive assets respond differently from defensive assets.

• Carry contraction may unfold over many months.
